Ten lights on your board.

Connect the tested ten-LED UNO setup to RTP’s zone records. The browser sends zone statuses over USB; the board shows them with lights.

Community records, not an official clearance.
This display does not detect red tide. A dark LED alone does not mean the water or shellfish are safe.

Connect your UNO

  1. Use the red_tide_zones sketch and its repository-owned RedTideZones library. The firmware guide includes setup instructions.
  2. Keep the ten LEDs on D13–D9, D7–D3, each with its own resistor and common GND. D8 is unused. Leave the buzzer, servo and ultrasonic sensor disconnected.
  3. Plug the board into your laptop. Close Arduino Serial Monitor and Plotter.
  4. Open the zone-status display in desktop Chrome or Edge, choose Connect zone UNO, then select your board.

Keep that display tab visible and your computer awake. Check both the source status and USB acknowledgment.

Fast blinking after plugging in?

Seven fast-blinking zone LEDs, D3 steadily on, and D5/D4 off is the stale pattern. It means the board needs fresh status frames. It is not a red-tide warning. Connect on the zone-status page; without valid frames, the powered board returns to this pattern after 15 seconds.

Older combined-hardware diagnostics

The separate LED/scanner/combined tools use different firmware and wiring. The combined sketch’s servo and sensor pins conflict with this ten-LED setup. Disconnect power before rewiring.
